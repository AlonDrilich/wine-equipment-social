# Free Distribution Playbook

> Written after the 2026-08-03 measurement: the Facebook Page has **1 follower** and 89+ published
> posts with zero measurable reach. Paid is off the table by the founder's decision, so this is the
> complete list of levers that cost nothing but time. **~20 minutes a week.**
>
> The content is already made and scheduled through Aug 31. This document is only about getting
> humans in front of it.

---

## Why the Page alone can't work
Facebook Page organic reach is under 2% in 2026, and 2% of one follower is nobody. A Page post is
seen by people who *already* follow you. Groups are the opposite: members get notified, and posts
from a stranger surface normally. That asymmetry is the entire opportunity here.

**The hard rule: Groups are human-only.** Never automate them — it is the fastest way to get the
account banned, and most winemaking groups ban overt promotion outright. Lead with the answer; the
brand follows.

Read each group's promo policy before the first post. Some allow a link if it's clearly helpful;
some allow none. When in doubt, answer with no link and let people ask.

### The weekly routine (15 minutes)
1. Search the groups for the week's live questions: *"what press"*, *"what size tank"*, *"304 or
   316L"*, *"crossflow worth it"*, *"used or new"*.
2. Answer **two** of them properly, from the replies below. No link unless it's obviously welcome.
3. If a reply gets traction, and only then, offer the reference card: *"I've got a sizing table for
   this if it helps — happy to send it."*

### Ready-to-paste replies
These are written to stand on their own as expertise. Each maps to a card in `media/assets/cards-v6/`
you can share **if asked**.

**Q — "What size tank do I need for X cases?"**
> Quick rule that has held up for me: about 1,000 L of red fermenter per ton of fruit (must, cap rise
> and headspace), and about 700 L of tank per ton for whites, since it's juice only. Then round up to
> the next catalogue size. The thing I'd push back on is buying one big tank for the annual volume —
> at most sizes you're better off with a set of smaller ones. You can keep parcels separate, and you
> fill closer to full, which means less headspace to fight all winter.
> *(card: `u1-ton-to-litre`)*

**Q — "Is crossflow worth it?"**
> Depends entirely on what you're putting through it. If you're polishing already-clean wine before
> the filler, plates and cartridges do that for a fraction of the capital. If you're pushing heavy
> lees, you want a dedicated lees filter — running that through your main crossflow is how you kill
> membranes you just paid for. Crossflow earns its money on bulk, turbid, post-ferment wine you want
> off in one pass with almost no oxygen pickup. Sizing sum is simple: litres to filter ÷ hours you'll
> actually run. 20,000 L over a ten-hour day is 2,000 L/hr minimum, and rated throughput is always
> measured on clean wine, so buy a step up.
> *(card: `u4-filter-tree`)*

**Q — "304 or 316L?"**
> For most tank bodies in a normal cellar, 304 is fine and meaningfully cheaper. 316L earns its
> premium where chlorides live — coastal air, hard or chlorinated water, aggressive sanitation, long
> contact with low-pH wine. The extra molybdenum is what resists the pitting. Honestly though, the
> bigger variable is weld and finish quality: a badly passivated 316L weld will pit before a clean
> 304 one. Ask whether the interior welds were back-purged, ground flush and passivated, and whether
> they can produce the material certs.
> *(card: `v4-steel-tree`)*

**Q — "Bigger press or is mine enough?"**
> Before buying bigger, check cycle time. A smaller press that fills, presses, empties and reloads
> faster will clear more fruit in a day than a larger one that sits between loads. Capacity tells you
> what fits in one load; cycle time tells you what actually gets finished. It's rarely on the first
> page of a quote — worth asking for it directly, along with the drainage design.
> *(card: `cmp-press` / `v6-press-versus`)*

**Q — "New or used?"**
> I'd split it by consequence. If the machine fails mid-crush and nothing really stops — storage
> tanks, hoses, fittings — used is easy money, often 40–60% off. If a failure costs you the day but
> there's a workaround, used is fine from a maker whose parts still exist. If a failure stops the
> vintage — press, destemmer, bottling line — buy it vetted with warranty and service. And if you do
> go used on a press, budget a membrane and a service visit up front; you'll still be ahead.
> *(card: `v5-used-tree`)*

**Q — "What throughput destemmer do I need?"**
> Size it to your worst morning, not your annual tonnage. Take the peak-day intake and divide by the
> hours you actually have — if 20 tons arrive and you want it cleared in six hours, that's 4 t/hr
> minimum. Then buy a step up, because rated throughput is measured on clean, dry, hand-picked fruit,
> and your real pick has MOG and sometimes rain in it.
> *(card: `v1-destemmer-sizing`)*

**Q — "Quote is in hL / kg/h / DN / bar — help?"**
> Rough conversions: 50 hL = 5,000 L. 3,000 kg/h ≈ 3.3 tons/hour. DN50 = 2 inch line. 2 bar ≈ 29 psi.
> And check the electrics — 400V/50Hz is standard in Europe; a US site needs 60Hz, which is not
> always a simple swap. Also worth asking whether a tank volume is usable or gross, because those are
> different numbers and only one of them holds wine.
> *(card: `u3-spec-decoder`)*

## 2. LinkedIn — founder-led, and free
The reels are already publishing to the Company Page. What actually generates B2B reach is a
**person**, not a page: personal profiles get roughly 8× the engagement.
- Daniel or Sophie posting 2× a week from their own profile — an observation from sourcing, not a
  brand post. The material is already written in `content/linkedin/week-01.md`.
- **Links go in the first comment**, never the post body (external links are suppressed).
- 15 minutes of commenting on winemakers' and suppliers' posts beats an extra post. Contextual
  comments out-convert cold DMs by a wide margin.

## 3. The site itself
The cheapest followers available: add follow/share buttons to wine.equipment, and put the social
links in the footer, the quote confirmation email, and the guide pages. Every guide reader is a
qualified potential follower who currently has no way to find the feed.

## 4. Trade moments (free to post around)
Unified Wine & Grape Symposium (Jan, Sacramento) · WIN Expo (Dec, Santa Rosa) · SIMEI (Nov, Milan) ·
Vinitech-Sifel (Dec, Bordeaux). Posting *about* an event picks up traffic from its own hashtag.
Northern-Hemisphere crush is Aug–Oct — that is now, and it is the moment this audience is paying
attention to equipment.

---

## Honest expectation
None of this scales like paid. Two good group answers a week might reach a few hundred genuinely
relevant people — which, against the current baseline of one follower, is the entire difference.
The content is done and scheduled; the only variable left is whether a human spends 20 minutes a
week putting it where winemakers actually are.
